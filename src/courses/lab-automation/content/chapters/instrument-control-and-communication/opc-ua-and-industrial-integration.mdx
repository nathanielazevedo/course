SiLA solves talking to a lab *instrument*. But an automated lab rarely lives alone — it sits inside a building of utilities, a manufacturing process, and an enterprise that wants to know what's happening. Connecting that wider world is a problem the factory floor solved years ago, and its standard has come to the lab: **OPC-UA**.

### Where it comes from

**OPC-UA** (*Open Platform Communications — Unified Architecture*) is the dominant standard for **industrial** automation. It's how programmable logic controllers (PLCs), sensors, robots, and the supervisory systems above them exchange data in factories and process plants. It is platform-independent, service-oriented, and built with security as a first-class concern.

It arrives in the lab through **biomanufacturing** and **Industry 4.0** — the drive to connect bioreactors, process equipment, and analytical instruments into one plant-wide, data-rich system that feeds enterprise software.

### The information model

SiLA describes a device as a flat set of Features. OPC-UA goes further: it represents everything as a rich, browsable **information model** — an **address space** of connected **nodes**. A node can be a variable (a temperature), an object (a pump), a method (start), or an event, and nodes link to each other to express structure and meaning. A client can **browse** this model at runtime to discover not just *what* data exists but *how it relates* — that a temperature reading belongs to a specific reactor, which belongs to a specific suite.

This semantic richness is what lets OPC-UA model an entire plant, not just one box.

### Client/server and pub/sub

OPC-UA supports two communication styles:

- **Client/server** — a client connects to a server, browses the address space, reads and writes values, calls methods, and **subscribes** to changes so it's notified when a value updates.
- **Publish/subscribe (pub/sub)** — servers publish data to many consumers at once, suited to high-volume telemetry and many-listener scenarios.

Security — authentication, encryption, and signing — is built into the protocol, which matters when instrument data crosses into regulated, networked enterprise systems.

### Where it sits: the integration layer

The cleanest way to place OPC-UA is by altitude. SiLA controls a device; OPC-UA integrates many systems into the enterprise stack above them.

### SiLA and OPC-UA together

These standards are complements, not rivals. A common pattern: SiLA drives the instruments, and a bridge exposes the resulting data to the plant over OPC-UA. The two worlds are converging directly through **LADS** (*Laboratory and Analytical Device Standard*), an official **OPC-UA companion specification** that defines a standard information model for lab and analytical devices — bringing lab instruments natively into the OPC-UA ecosystem.

The practical rule of thumb:

- **Driver / command-response** — one device, lowest level, when nothing standardized exists.
- **SiLA 2** — standardized control of lab instruments without bespoke drivers.
- **OPC-UA (incl. LADS)** — integrating instruments and process equipment into the plant and enterprise.

With device control and integration in hand, the system can not only run an instrument but feed everything it produces into the data and sample-tracking layer — the subject of the next chapter.
