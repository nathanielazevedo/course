Every plate, tube, and well moving through the systems covered so far holds something real: a specific sample, from a specific source, with its own history. Losing track of which sample is which — even briefly — can make an otherwise perfect run scientifically worthless. This section is about what "identity" means for a sample and why it has to be actively maintained.

### What a sample's identity actually consists of

A sample's identity is more than a name — it's the full set of facts that make one sample distinguishable from every other: where it came from (a patient, a cell line, a reagent lot), when it was collected or created, what's happened to it since (diluted, split, frozen and thawed), and where it currently physically sits. Two tubes that look identical, sitting in the same rack, can hold samples with completely different identities — the only thing that actually distinguishes them is the identity recorded against each one.

### Identity has to survive every physical transformation

The hard part isn't recording identity once — it's carrying it correctly through every operation a sample undergoes. When a sample is split into multiple aliquots, each resulting tube needs an identity that traces back to the original *and* distinguishes it from its siblings. When two reagents are combined into a new well, that well's identity is now a function of both inputs, not either one alone. A system that tracks identity only at the start and end of a workflow, without updating it at every transformation in between, ends up with data that looks complete but is quietly disconnected from what actually happened physically.

### Lineage: identity plus history

Beyond just "which sample is this," most systems also need **lineage** — the full chain of what a sample was derived from and what was done to it along the way. Lineage answers questions identity alone can't: if a result looks wrong, which source sample, which reagent lot, which specific run produced it? Without lineage, a bad result is a dead end; with it, a bad result is the start of an investigation that can actually be traced back to its cause.

### Why this is a prerequisite, not a feature

Everything explored in this chapter's remaining sections — barcodes, LIMS, capturing instrument data — exists to serve this one underlying need: keeping identity and lineage accurate as a sample moves through a system that, left unmanaged, has no idea one tube's contents from another's. Get sample identity wrong, and no amount of instrument precision or scheduling sophistication elsewhere in this course produces trustworthy results.
